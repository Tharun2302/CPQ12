# Zenop.ai CPQ — End-to-End Workflow Video Script

**Length:** about 10–11 minutes
**Audience:** sales reps, approvers and anyone new to the CPQ app
**Format:** each scene has what to show on screen and what to say (voice-over)

---

## Scene 1 — Introduction (0:00–0:40)

**On screen:** Zenop.ai landing page.

**Voice-over:**
> Welcome to Zenop.ai, our CPQ tool built to make your work faster and easier.
> With Zenop.ai you can create quotes, generate SOW agreements, and more, much like tools such as PandaDoc.
> In this video we'll follow one deal from start to finish: opening it from HubSpot, configuring the migration, pricing it, generating the agreement, getting it approved by the Team Lead, Technical and Legal teams, and finally getting it signed by the customer.

---

## Scene 2 — Accessing Zenop.ai from HubSpot (0:40–1:50)

**On screen:** HubSpot. Open any deal and scroll down the left sidebar to find the **Zenop.ai** link.

**Voice-over:**
> Zenop.ai is opened directly from HubSpot, so every quote starts from a real deal.
> Open any deal in HubSpot and scroll down. You'll find Zenop.ai in the deal details.

**On screen:** If Zenop.ai isn't there: under **About this deal**, click **Actions → Customize properties**, then **Add properties**, select **Zenop.ai**, and click **Save**. Zenop.ai now appears in the deal view.

**Voice-over:**
> If you don't see Zenop.ai, you can add it in a few clicks. Under "About this deal", select Actions, then Customize properties.
> Click Add properties, select "Zenop.ai", and click Save.
> Zenop.ai now appears in the deal view. You only have to do this once.

**On screen:** Click the **Zenop.ai** link. The Microsoft sign-in page opens; sign in with the CloudFuze account.

**Voice-over:**
> To open the deal in Zenop.ai, just click the Zenop.ai link.
> You'll be taken to the Microsoft sign-in page. Sign in with your CloudFuze Microsoft account.

---

## Scene 3 — Deal page (1:50–2:40)

**On screen:** After sign-in, Zenop.ai opens on the **Deal** tab. Point out Deal Information (deal ID, name, stage, close date), then the Contact and Company details.

**Voice-over:**
> After signing in, you land on the Deal page in Zenop.ai, and the deal is already loaded from HubSpot.
> Here you can see the deal ID, deal name, stage and close date, which come from the deal you just clicked.
> The customer's primary contact and company are fetched live from HubSpot, so even if a contact was added or renamed after the deal was created, you'll see the latest details.
> You don't need to type any customer information. It carries through automatically into the quote and the agreement.
> Next, let's configure what the customer is buying.

---

## Scene 4 — Configure (2:40–4:10)

**On screen:** **Configure** tab. Choose a category (Content, Messaging or Email), pick a combination such as *SLACK TO TEAMS* or *BOX TO MICROSOFT (ONEDRIVE/SHAREPOINT)*, and fill in users, data size or message count, and duration. Briefly show the Migrate option picker (Multi-Combination, Overage Agreement, Data Sprawl).

**Voice-over:**
> On the Configure tab we describe the migration project.
> First pick the category: Content, Messaging or Email. Then select the combination, meaning the source and destination, for example Slack to Teams, or Box to OneDrive and SharePoint.
> Next we enter the project details: the number of users, the data size in GB or the number of messages, the instance type, and the duration in months.
> If the deal covers more than one migration, use Multi-Combination to add several combinations to a single quote. Overage Agreement and Data Sprawl can be ticked as well, and they're added into the same agreement.
> As we type, CPQ prices the project in real time.

**On screen:** The pricing cards, with the plans side by side and the totals updating.

**Voice-over:**
> Below the form, the plans are shown side by side with their prices, so the rep and the customer can compare options and choose the right one.
> You can also apply a discount here. Discounts are capped by the company's discount policy, which an admin sets, so a quote can't go out below the approved limit.

---

## Scene 5 — Quote and agreement generation (4:10–5:40)

**On screen:** **Quote** tab. Show the auto-selected template, the start, effective and expiry date pickers, the optional scope document upload, then click **Generate**. The "Agreement Generated!" preview appears.

**Voice-over:**
> Now we move to the Quote tab.
> CPQ automatically selects the right agreement template for the combination we configured. We set the project start date, the effective date and the quote expiry date. If there's a custom scope, we can attach it as a Word document.
> The Placeholder Replacement Preview shows exactly how our data will fill the template: customer name, pricing, users, duration and so on.
> Click Generate, and in a few seconds the full agreement is ready, with the matching exhibits for each combination merged in: what's included in the migration and what isn't.

**On screen:** Open the agreement in the built-in Word editor, make a small edit and save.

**Voice-over:**
> If anything needs adjusting, the agreement opens in a built-in Word editor, so we can edit it with all of its formatting intact.
> Once we're happy with it, we send it for approval.

---

## Scene 6 — Send for approval and prepare signatures (5:40–6:40)

**On screen:** Click **Send for Approval**, choose the team, and show the approval chain (Step 1 Team Lead, Step 2 Technical Team, Step 3 Legal Team). The app moves to the place-fields screen: add the customer signer, drag signature, name and date fields onto the signature page, then start the workflow.

**Voice-over:**
> Clicking "Send for Approval" shows the approval chain. It's sequential: first the Team Lead of the selected team, then the Technical team, then Legal.
> Before the workflow starts, we prepare the document for signing. We add the customer signer as a recipient, go to the signature page, and drag the signature, name and date fields into place.
> Then we start the workflow. From this point the agreement is locked: it can't be downloaded until every approval is done, so an unapproved version never reaches the customer.

---

## Scene 7 — Approvals (6:40–8:10)

**On screen:** The approval email arriving, then the Team Lead dashboard with Approve, Deny and comment. Then the Technical approval link showing "Sign in to approve", Microsoft sign-in, approve. Then the Legal approval.

**Voice-over:**
> Each approver gets an email with a link to the document.
> The Team Lead reviews it first and can approve, deny or leave a comment.
> Next is the Technical team. For security, technical approvers must sign in with their CloudFuze Microsoft account before they can approve, and the system records exactly who approved.
> For Overage deals, the Technical step is skipped automatically after the Team Lead approves.
> Finally, Legal reviews the agreement and gives the last approval.

**On screen:** **Approval** tab, showing a workflow card with each stage, its time taken, and the total.

**Voice-over:**
> Back on the Approval tab, the sales rep can track progress at any time: which step it's on, who approved, and how long each stage took.
> If plans change, the requester can cancel the approval, and the approvers' links will show that it was cancelled.

---

## Scene 8 — E-signature (8:10–9:40)

**On screen:** After Legal approves, the completion email arrives and the signer's signature email appears. The customer opens the link, reviews the agreement, signs and submits.

**Voice-over:**
> As soon as Legal approves, two things happen automatically: the rep gets a completion email, and the agreement goes out to the customer for signature. Nobody has to send it by hand.
> The customer opens the link, reviews the agreement, and signs in the fields we placed earlier.

**On screen:** **e sign status** tab. The agreement moves through Sent, Viewed and Signed to Completed. Download the signed PDF.

**Voice-over:**
> On the e-sign status page we can follow every agreement: Sent, Viewed, Signed, Completed, or Declined.
> When everyone has signed, the executed PDF is stored in CPQ and can be downloaded from here, and the completion email goes out.

**On screen:** **Manual e-sign** tab, with the signing method picker (CPQ e-signature or Zoho Sign).

**Voice-over:**
> For documents that don't need the approval workflow, the Manual e-sign tab lets you upload a document, add recipients, place fields and send it directly.
> You can choose the signing method: CPQ's built-in e-signature, which is the default, or Zoho Sign.

---

## Scene 9 — Behind the scenes: admin tabs (9:40–10:25)

**On screen:** Quick tour of **Templates**, **Exhibits**, **Documents** and **Admin Settings**.

**Voice-over:**
> A quick look at the tabs that power all of this.
> Templates holds the agreement templates for each combination. Exhibits holds the included and not-included exhibits that get merged into agreements, and they can be redlined right here. Documents keeps every generated agreement in one place.
> In Admin Settings, admins set the maximum discount policy and manage approval admins.

---

## Scene 10 — Wrap-up (10:25–11:00)

**On screen:** A simple flow graphic:
**HubSpot Deal → Microsoft Sign-in → Deal Page → Configure → Price → Generate Agreement → Team Lead → Technical → Legal → Customer e-Sign → Signed PDF**

**Voice-over:**
> And that's the complete journey: from a HubSpot deal, to a configured and priced migration, to a generated agreement, through Team Lead, Technical and Legal approval, to a signed contract, all in one place.
> Faster quotes, consistent pricing, controlled approvals, and no chasing signatures.
> Thanks for watching.

---

### Recording tips
- Use a test deal with realistic data, and don't show real customer names or prices.
- Have the approver and signer inboxes open in separate browser profiles so you can switch between them smoothly.
- Approval emails can land in Microsoft 365 quarantine or Junk. Release them, or allowlist the sender, before recording.
- Record each scene separately and join them in editing. Scene timings are a guide, not a rule.
